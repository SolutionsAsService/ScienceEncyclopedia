Physics

  • Newton's laws of motionThree statements about force and motion that explain most everyday mechanics.
  • Conservation of energyEnergy changes form but, in an isolated system, its total stays the same.
  • The laws of thermodynamicsFour rules about heat, work, energy and the direction in which processes run.
  • Light and the electromagnetic spectrumVisible light is one narrow band of a continuous spectrum of electromagnetic waves.
  • RelativitySpecial relativity changes how space and time fit together at high speed; general relativity treats gravity as the curvature of spacetime.
  • Quantum mechanicsThe theory of matter and light at atomic scales, where energy comes in discrete amounts and particles behave like waves.
  • Electric charge, current and Ohm's lawElectric current is a flow of charge, and in many materials the current is proportional to the voltage.

Chemistry

  • The atomThe smallest unit of an element: a dense nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons.
  • The periodic law and the periodic tableWhen the elements are ordered by atomic number, their properties repeat at regular intervals.
  • Chemical bondsThe attractions that hold atoms together in compounds, metals and molecules.
  • The mole and Avogadro's constantThe mole is the chemist's counting unit: a fixed, very large number of particles.
  • Acids, bases and pHA scale for how acidic or basic a water solution is, based on its hydrogen ion concentration.
  • Balancing chemical equationsAtoms are neither created nor destroyed in a reaction, so both sides of an equation must contain the same atoms.
  • Oxidation and reductionReactions in which electrons move from one substance to another.

Biology

  • The cellThe basic unit of structure and function of living things.
  • DNAThe molecule that stores hereditary information in nearly all living things.
  • Heredity and geneticsHow traits are passed from parents to offspring.
  • Evolution by natural selectionHow populations change over generations and how species share common ancestors.
  • PhotosynthesisHow plants, algae and some bacteria turn light, water and carbon dioxide into sugar and oxygen.
  • Cellular respirationHow cells release the energy in food as ATP.

Earth and space

  • Plate tectonicsEarth's outer shell is split into plates that slowly move over the layer beneath.
  • The water cycleThe continuous movement of water between oceans, air, land and living things.
  • The Solar SystemThe Sun and everything held by its gravity, including eight planets.
