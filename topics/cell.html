The cell

The basic unit of structure and function of living things.

Cell theory, developed in the 1800s by Matthias Schleiden, Theodor Schwann and Rudolf Virchow, says that all living things are made of cells and that cells arise from existing cells. Prokaryotic cells, bacteria and archaea, have no nucleus. Eukaryotic cells, the cells of animals, plants, fungi and protists, keep their DNA in a nucleus and contain organelles such as mitochondria.

Plant cells have a cell wall and, in photosynthetic tissue, chloroplasts. Animal cells have neither.
