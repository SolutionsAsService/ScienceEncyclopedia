The laws of thermodynamics

Four rules about heat, work, energy and the direction in which processes run.

The zeroth law says that two bodies each in thermal equilibrium with a third are in equilibrium with each other, which is what makes temperature well defined. The first law is conservation of energy applied to heat and work. The second law says that the total entropy of an isolated system does not decrease, which is why heat flows from hot to cold and not the other way unprompted. The third law says that entropy approaches a constant as temperature approaches absolute zero, and that absolute zero cannot be reached in a finite number of steps.

Entropy is often described as disorder; a more careful reading is that it counts how many microscopic arrangements are consistent with what we see at large scale.
