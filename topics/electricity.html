Electric charge, current and Ohm's law

Electric current is a flow of charge, and in many materials the current is proportional to the voltage.

Charge comes in two kinds; like charges repel and unlike charges attract, with a force that falls with the square of the distance (Coulomb's law). Current is the rate of flow of charge, measured in amperes. In many conductors, at constant temperature, the current I through a component is proportional to the voltage V across it, and V = IR defines the resistance R in ohms.

Not every component is ohmic. A diode or a light bulb filament does not obey V = IR with a constant R.
