Quantum mechanics

The theory of matter and light at atomic scales, where energy comes in discrete amounts and particles behave like waves.

Light is absorbed and emitted in packets called photons, each with energy E = hν. Electrons bound in atoms can only have certain energies, which is why each element emits and absorbs light at characteristic wavelengths. Particles also show wave behaviour: a beam of electrons sent through two narrow slits builds up an interference pattern one electron at a time.

The uncertainty principle says that position and momentum cannot both be known exactly: the product of their uncertainties is at least ħ/2, where ħ = h/2π. This is a property of nature and not a limit of our instruments.
