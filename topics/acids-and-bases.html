Acids, bases and pH

A scale for how acidic or basic a water solution is, based on its hydrogen ion concentration.

In the Brønsted–Lowry picture an acid gives away a proton and a base accepts one. The pH of a solution is the negative base-10 logarithm of the hydrogen ion activity, in practice often the concentration in moles per litre. Each pH unit is a factor of ten, so a solution of pH 3 is ten times more acidic than one of pH 4.

At 25 °C pure water is neutral at pH 7. Many everyday solutions fall between 0 and 14, but strong acids and bases can go outside that range.
