Biology

DNA

The molecule that stores hereditary information in nearly all living things.

DNA is two strands wound into a double helix, as worked out in 1953 by James Watson and Francis Crick using X-ray diffraction data from Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins. Each strand is a chain of units called nucleotides, each carrying one of four bases: adenine, thymine, guanine or cytosine. Adenine pairs with thymine and guanine with cytosine, so each strand carries the information needed to rebuild the other.

Genes are stretches of DNA whose sequence specifies a protein or a functional RNA. The sequence is read in sets of three bases, each set coding for an amino acid.
