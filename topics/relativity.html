Relativity

Special relativity changes how space and time fit together at high speed; general relativity treats gravity as the curvature of spacetime.

Special relativity rests on two ideas: the laws of physics are the same in every frame moving at constant velocity, and the speed of light in a vacuum is the same for all observers. From them follow time dilation, length contraction, and the equivalence of mass and energy, E = mc² for a body at rest.

General relativity extends this to gravity: mass and energy curve spacetime, and free-falling bodies follow the straightest paths through it. The satellite navigation system relies on both theories, because its clocks run at slightly different rates from clocks on the ground.
