Earth and space

Plate tectonics

Earth's outer shell is split into plates that slowly move over the layer beneath.

The lithosphere, the rigid crust and uppermost mantle, is broken into plates that move over the softer asthenosphere. Plates move apart at mid-ocean ridges, collide at convergent boundaries, where one may sink beneath the other, and slide past each other at transform faults. Most earthquakes and volcanoes lie along these boundaries.

Alfred Wegener proposed continental drift in 1912, but it was accepted only in the 1960s, when seafloor spreading and magnetic stripes in ocean-floor rock supplied a mechanism and evidence.
