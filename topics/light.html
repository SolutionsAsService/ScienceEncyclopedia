Light and the electromagnetic spectrum

Visible light is one narrow band of a continuous spectrum of electromagnetic waves.

Radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are the same kind of wave, an oscillating electric and magnetic field, differing in wavelength. In a vacuum all of them travel at the same speed, and wavelength λ and frequency ν are linked by c = λν. Shorter wavelengths carry more energy per photon.

People see roughly from violet to red. The edges of that band vary between people and between sources, so any quoted range is approximate.
