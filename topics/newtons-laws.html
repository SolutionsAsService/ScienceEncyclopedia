Newton's laws of motion

Three statements about force and motion that explain most everyday mechanics.

The first law says a body keeps its state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless a net force acts on it. The second says the net force on a body equals the rate of change of its momentum; for a body of constant mass this is F = ma, so the same force gives a lighter body a larger acceleration. The third says that when one body pushes on another, the second pushes back on the first with an equal and opposite force.

The two forces in the third law act on different bodies, which is why they do not cancel. The laws work well for ordinary speeds and sizes. They need replacing by special relativity near the speed of light and by quantum mechanics at atomic scales.
