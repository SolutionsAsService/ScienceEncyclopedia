The mole and Avogadro's constant

The mole is the chemist's counting unit: a fixed, very large number of particles.

One mole of any substance contains the same number of entities, whether atoms, molecules or ions. Since 2019 that number is fixed exactly and defines the mole. The molar mass of a substance, in grams per mole, is close to its relative atomic or molecular mass, which lets a chemist weigh out a known number of particles.

Before 2019 the mole was defined through twelve grams of carbon-12. That is now only approximately true.
