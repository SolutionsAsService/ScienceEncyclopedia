The periodic law and the periodic table

When the elements are ordered by atomic number, their properties repeat at regular intervals.

Dmitri Mendeleev published his table in 1869, ordering the elements mainly by atomic mass and leaving gaps where the pattern implied an element was missing. He predicted properties for three of them, which were later found and called gallium, scandium and germanium. In 1913 Henry Moseley showed that the true ordering variable is atomic number, the count of protons.

Columns (groups) share chemical behaviour because their atoms have similar outer-electron arrangements. Rows (periods) correspond to the filling of successive electron shells. See the full table on the elements page.
