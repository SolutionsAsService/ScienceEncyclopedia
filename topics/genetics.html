Biology

Heredity and genetics

How traits are passed from parents to offspring.

Gregor Mendel's work with pea plants, published in 1866, showed that inheritance works through discrete units, now called genes, which come in variants called alleles. An organism usually carries two copies of each gene, and in the simplest cases one allele is dominant over the other.

Mitosis copies a cell into two genetically identical cells for growth and repair. Meiosis halves the number of chromosome sets to make eggs and sperm, and shuffles the alleles, so offspring differ from their parents and from each other.
